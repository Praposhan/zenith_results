The 8.8-Hour Rule Nobody Told You About Your Coffee Habit

2026 · By Geethika Babu

Everyone has a personal theory about when their "last coffee of the day" should be. In 2023, a team led by Carissa Gardiner actually ran the numbers.

What the meta-analysis found

Published in Sleep Medicine Reviews, the study pooled 24 studies on caffeine's effect on nighttime sleep. The results were specific: caffeine consumption reduced total sleep time by an average of 45 minutes and sleep efficiency by 7%, while increasing the time it took to fall asleep by 9 minutes and time spent awake during the night by 12 minutes. It also shifted sleep architecture itself — more light sleep, less deep sleep. Based on these effects, the researchers proposed a concrete cutoff: to avoid measurable sleep loss, a standard 250 mL cup of coffee should be consumed at least 8.8 hours before bedtime.

Why "I can handle caffeine late" isn't quite the flex it sounds like

Caffeine's half-life varies enormously between people — anywhere from 2 to 10 hours — influenced by genetics, liver function, and even hormonal contraceptive use. Some people genuinely do metabolize it faster than others. But the studies included people who felt fine falling asleep after afternoon coffee, and their actual sleep architecture was disrupted anyway, measured objectively, not just by how rested they felt. Subjective "I slept fine" and objective "your sleep was fine" turned out to be two different things.
