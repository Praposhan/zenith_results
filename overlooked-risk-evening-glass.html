The Overlooked Risk Factor Sitting in Your Evening Glass of Wine

2026 · By Geethika Babu

Most people think of alcohol and sleep apnea as unrelated topics. The research says otherwise, and the connection is mechanical, not incidental.

What a meta-analysis of 21 studies found

A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis published in Sleep Medicine, led by Evangelia Simou and colleagues at the University of Nottingham, examined the relationship between alcohol consumption and sleep apnea risk across 21 studies. The pooled result: higher alcohol consumption was associated with a 25% increased risk of sleep apnea. The mechanism is straightforward — alcohol relaxes the muscles of the upper airway, the same muscles whose collapse during sleep is what defines an apnea event in the first place.

Why this matters beyond just "drink less"

This isn't a case for total abstinence — it's a case for noticing a pattern. If someone already has other risk factors for sleep-disordered breathing (loud snoring, daytime fatigue, a higher BMI), evening alcohol is one of the more modifiable pieces sitting in plain sight, layered directly onto a structural risk that already exists.
