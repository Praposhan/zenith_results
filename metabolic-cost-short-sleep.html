The Quiet Metabolic Cost of Short Sleep

2 min read · PRAPOSHAN Journal

Sleep doesn't usually make the list when people think about metabolic health — diet and exercise take up all the attention. The research suggests that's a real gap.

Sleep and diabetes risk

A well-known meta-analysis by Cappuccio and colleagues examined the relationship between sleep quantity and quality and the incidence of type 2 diabetes, finding a consistent association between insufficient sleep and elevated diabetes risk across the pooled studies. More recent research using NHANES data — the large U.S. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey — has refined the picture further, describing an inverted U-shaped relationship between weekday sleep duration and insulin resistance, with roughly seven hours appearing to be the sweet spot, and both shorter and notably longer sleep associated with worse insulin resistance markers.

Not just how much — but how consistent

This is part of why sleep coaching that focuses purely on "get more hours" misses part of the picture. The research increasingly points to a combination of adequate duration and consistency as the more complete metabolic story, not total hours in isolation.

The ZENITH Assessment looks at sleep duration, efficiency, and consistency together — not just one number in isolation.

References
