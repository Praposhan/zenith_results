The Insomnia Treatment More Effective Than Pills (That Almost Nobody Uses)

2026 · By Geethika Babu

If you asked most people how to fix chronic insomnia, "see a doctor for sleeping pills" would be the default answer. Sleep medicine's own guidelines say otherwise.

The first-line treatment isn't a pill

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) — a structured approach combining behavioral techniques like stimulus control and sleep restriction with cognitive reframing — is recommended as the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia by major sleep medicine guidelines. A comparative effectiveness review in the American Psychiatric Association's journal Focus concluded that CBT-I is at least as effective as sleep medications, and its benefits tend to be more durable over time — without the tolerance and dependence risks that come with long-term hypnotic use.

It doesn't just fix sleep — it moves mental health too

A 2021 meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials by Scott and colleagues, published in Sleep Medicine Reviews, went further: it found that improving sleep quality through interventions like CBT-I produced measurable downstream improvements in mental health outcomes. This wasn't just correlation dressed up as causation — these were controlled trials specifically designed to test whether fixing sleep changes mental health outcomes, and the answer was yes.

So why doesn't everyone use it?

Partly access, partly awareness — a full CBT-I course typically requires a trained therapist and several structured sessions, which is a bigger lift than picking up a prescription. But for something with this much evidence behind it, it's strikingly under-used relative to medication.
