The Two-Way Street Between Sleep and Mental Health

2 min read · PRAPOSHAN Journal

Anxiety and poor sleep tend to travel together, and the usual assumption is that anxiety causes the sleeplessness. The research suggests the arrow points both ways — and that fixing sleep can move the needle on its own.

Evidence that sleep improvement drives mental health, not just the reverse

A 2021 meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials by Scott and colleagues, published in Sleep Medicine Reviews, specifically tested whether interventions designed to improve sleep quality produced measurable downstream improvements in mental health outcomes. Because these were controlled trials — not just observational studies tracking two things that happen to correlate — the design allowed researchers to say something closer to causal: improving sleep quality led to better mental health outcomes, not merely alongside them.

What this means practically

It doesn't mean sleep is the only lever, or that every mental health concern resolves once sleep improves. But it does mean sleep isn't just a symptom to wait out until the "real" issue is addressed — it's an active lever in its own right, worth addressing directly and in parallel with other support, not after it.

This is exactly why Praposhan pairs its coaching with Calm to Rise, a direct connection to a licensed psychologist — because the two genuinely work better addressed together than in sequence.

References

  1. Scott, A. J., Webb, T. L., Martyn-St James, M., Rowse, G., & Weich, S. (2021). Improving sleep quality leads to better mental health: A meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. Sleep Medicine Reviews, 60, 101556.
