The Science-Backed Case for a Midday Nap at Work

2 min read · PRAPOSHAN Journal

Napping at work still carries a stigma in a lot of professional cultures — as if it signals laziness rather than strategy. The cognitive research doesn't support that stigma.

What the meta-analyses show

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis pooling 11 studies found that a daytime nap — averaging just under an hour — measurably improved cognitive performance compared to no nap, with the strongest effect on alertness specifically. Naps taken earlier in the afternoon, before 1pm, produced better cognitive outcomes than later ones, and the benefits lasted up to two hours after waking. A separate, larger 2022 meta-analysis by Leong and colleagues, pooling 60 samples across 54 studies, reached a similar conclusion: afternoon naps meaningfully boost attention and memory task performance, an effect that was particularly pronounced in people who were already sleep-restricted.

The nuance worth knowing

Timing and duration both matter — a nap too late in the day, or too long, risks sleep inertia (that groggy, worse-than-before feeling right after waking) and can interfere with nighttime sleep. The research consistently points to shorter, earlier-afternoon naps as the sweet spot, not an open invitation to sleep whenever convenient.

For people already running a sleep deficit, small structural changes like this are often the fastest lever to pull — exactly the kind of thing a ZENITH Score points toward directly.
